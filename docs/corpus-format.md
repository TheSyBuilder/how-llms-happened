# Corpus format

LLM Lineage keeps research evidence separate from interface copy. The canonical corpus is [data/corpus.json](../data/corpus.json), and its structural contract is [schemas/corpus.schema.json](../schemas/corpus.schema.json).

The current schema is `0.8.0`. As of 2026-09-05, the checked-in corpus contains 56 works, 166 versions, 187 ideas, 454 sources, 770 claims, 87 relationships, and 110 learning objectives.

The checked-in corpus covers Markov’s 1913 *Eugene Onegin* study, Shannon’s 1948 and 1951 papers, Good’s 1953 frequency-of-frequencies estimator as statistical context, Jelinek, Bahl, and Mercer’s 1975 statistical speech decoder, Jelinek and Mercer’s 1980 treatment of interpolation and deleted estimation for sparse Markov sources, and the reciprocally cross-referenced 1982 handbook chapter that applies the method to a word-trigram model. It records the differently titled 1983 IEEE article, with its different author order, as a journal companion to that chapter after a claim-level comparison, adds Hinton’s 1986 learned distributed representations as a neural representation branch, and adds Katz’s 1987 recursive backoff estimator while keeping its inherited Good–Turing machinery distinct from Katz’s stated contribution. Elman’s April 1988 technical report and canonical March 1990 article contribute a prediction-trained recurrent-state branch; Miikkulainen and Dyer’s July 1991 article then builds a separately supervised modular neural NLP system around recurrent state and a task-learned global word lexicon. Its case-role and script outputs are not normalized language-model probabilities. Schmidhuber’s separate 1993 article establishes an earlier neural character-probability and arithmetic-coding node; the 1994–1996 Schmidhuber–Heil family retains that mechanism while adding codecs, tests, and diagnostics. The classical branch continues through Kneser and Ney’s 1995 context-diversity fallback distribution to Chen and Goodman’s 1998 interpolated, multi-discount extension. A separate recurrent branch adds Hochreiter and Schmidhuber’s 1995 technical report and canonical 1997 journal article, which define constant-error memory with input and output gates while keeping the original cell distinct from later learned retention and cell-state-to-gate variants. Gers, Schmidhuber, and Cummins’s 1999/2000 work then replaces the original fixed self-connection with learned state retention and reset. Bengio and Bengio’s 1999/2000 work contributes an ordered neural estimator for high-dimensional joint distributions. Xu and Rudnicky independently evaluate a direct one-word, no-hidden-layer softmax bigram in October 2000; Bengio et al.’s 2000 conference paper and canonical 2003 journal article instead combine learned word representations, a multiword feed-forward predictor, full-vocabulary normalization, the earlier joint-distribution mechanism, and a documented neural/interpolated-trigram mixture. Schwenk and Gauvain’s May 2002 branch explicitly extends that projection-based approach into a hybrid 64,000-word speech recognizer using a probability-preserving shortlist and sequential reuse of outputs requested for the same context. Gers, Schraudolph, and Schmidhuber’s August 2002 paper separately adds cell-state-to-gate peepholes to the forget-gated cell. Mikolov et al.’s September 2010 paper then combines the Elman recurrence it names with normalized neural word prediction, speech-rescoring mixtures, and optional online adaptation, while preserving one-step temporal credit assignment and long-context limitations. Sundermeyer et al.’s September 2012 paper applies a composite cell containing the original gated-memory core, adaptive retention, and cell-state-to-gate links to a word-level model; the correspondence is a qualified cross-source inference rather than evidence of identical implementations. The paper also tests projection and class-factored outputs and interpolates the result with a much larger count model for speech recognition. Graves’s August 2013–June 2014 preprint family combines stacked recurrent next-step generation with mixture-density handwriting prediction and a text-conditioned, unnormalized Gaussian-mixture soft window whose positive cumulative location increments force every window center forward. Paper versions, code snapshots, model checkpoints, datasets, and companion reports remain different artifact classes even when one work links them. A source with a `version-boundary` role may document a related release or publication but cannot establish promoted claims, chronology, or lineage for the work’s paper-version family. This is why GPT-2’s later checkpoint pages remain provenance while its report claims use the 24-leaf technical report, why Transformer-XL’s canonical claims use ACL folios or structural anchors in the official supplement, and why current TFDS C4 and later T5.1.1 documentation cannot silently revise launch C4 1.0.0 or original T5.

Six audited branches now sit between recurrent translation attention and the Transformer convergence record. Sukhbaatar et al. repeat differentiable reads across external-memory hops; Luong, Pham, and Manning compare global and local recurrent cross-attention; Cheng, Dong, and Lapata place causal same-sequence attention inside recurrent state updates; and Parikh et al. factor recurrence-free sentence-pair attention into independent Attend, Compare, and Aggregate stages. ByteNet supplies a separate attention-free branch whose masked and unmasked dilated convolutions preserve source resolution and shorten distant-position paths while generation remains autoregressive. ConvS2S supplies the contiguous-convolution branch, retaining dot-product source attention as repeated decoder-layer hops. Among those six pre-Transformer branches, only ConvS2S has a direct incoming edge to Transformer, narrowly scoped to the cited encoder–decoder attention route and learned positional-embedding alternative; ByteNet and the other branches remain documented context or comparison rather than adopted Transformer mechanisms.

The first post-Transformer bridge deliberately retains two contemporaneous recurrent transfer routes. ELMo trains separate forward and backward autoregressive objectives, can optionally adapt the biLM on unlabeled task-domain text, then freezes it for supervised tasks and learns task-specific mixtures over a context-independent character token layer and contextual recurrent layers. ULMFiT likewise adapts a general autoregressive LM on target-domain text, but transfers its parameters into the classifier and fine-tunes the supervised stack with controlled rates and unfreezing. ULMFiT directly cites and contrasts the fixed-feature route, which supports a noncausal `addresses` edge; it does not establish derivation, priority, or general superiority. Neither work uses Transformer self-attention or masked-language-model training.

The next two records are distinct Transformer pretraining branches. The 2018 OpenAI preprint—called GPT-1 here only as disclosed later shorthand—uses masked decoder self-attention for left-to-right language modeling, then adapts all pretrained parameters to each labeled task. BERT instead uses an encoder with deep bidirectional context, corrupts selected input tokens under a masked-language-model objective, and jointly trains a next-sentence classifier before full-model task adaptation. Its arXiv v1, arXiv v2, and canonical NAACL article retain separate result, appendix, and provenance boundaries. BERT’s bibliography calls the Radford paper a technical report, but the inspected Radford artifact identifies itself as a preprint; the corpus preserves the source artifact’s own label.

Two later 2019 records test different consequences of that fork. XLNet keeps Transformer self-attention, explicitly integrates Transformer-XL’s detached recurrence and relative positions, and addresses BERT’s corruption objective with sampled permutation factorizations, target-aware content/query streams, and partial prediction with span-based target sampling; permutation changes the factorization and attention mask, not the visible token order. RoBERTa instead retains BERT’s encoder, masked-token objective, and full-model adaptation while varying the training recipe. Its adoption of Radford et al.’s byte-level BPE is a narrowly documented tokenizer inheritance, and neither the BERT nor GPT-2 relationship transfers a decoder-only architecture, causal objective, or source results.

T5 is a third convergence route rather than another encoder-only or causal-decoder pretraining record. It returns to a Transformer encoder–decoder, uses one text-to-text interface and scalar bucketed self-attention position biases, reconstructs sentinel-delimited spans, and compares C4 filtering and repetition, supervised/unsupervised mixing, fine-tuning, and scale. Its original final checkpoints include supervised downstream examples and task-specific fine-tuning. The Transformer and BERT edges preserve only target-cited architecture, denoising, and baseline-scale substrates; they do not move T5’s C4 pipeline, sentinel implementation, fine-tuning route, mixture, scale, or results backward.

## What the corpus represents

The format separates six record types:

1. **Works** hold a display citation, inclusion rationale, chronology, versions, limitations, and beginner/expert significance. Each version owns its title, ordered authors, and machine-readable bibliography, including structured series, chapter, container editors, publisher, publication places, and identifiers when relevant.
2. **Ideas** name the mechanisms, formalisms, findings, objectives, problems, or constraints that connect works.
3. **Sources** identify the exact publisher record, original text, search snippet, translation, reprint, or archive consulted, with an access date and version scope.
4. **Claims** are atomic statements with a directness label, confidence, source locator, limitations, and uncertainty.
5. **Relationships** connect works or ideas using both a connection type and an independent evidence class.
6. **Learning objectives** define different beginner and expert outcomes over the same work, idea, and claim IDs.

This separation lets the eventual interface change its teaching path without creating a second factual history.

## Dates and versions

Every chronology event records a value and its precision:

```json
{
  "value": "1948-07",
  "precision": "month",
  "calendar": "gregorian"
}
```

A work’s `chronology.orderingEventId` selects the event used for timeline order. Other events remain visible rather than being collapsed into one date. Every event also carries `dateEvidence`; validation rejects a date whose precision is finer than the cited record supports.
Historical dates that have not been calendar-normalized use `source-as-printed` with a note instead of silently pretending to be Gregorian.

## Evidence locators

Every claim requires at least one source location. Locators use the numbering printed in the cited source, not a browser or PDF viewer’s page index. When an inspected manuscript has no printed folios, its source records a `pdf-page` leaf-count envelope, but promoted locators omit page fields and use stable section, equation, figure, table, or paragraph anchors instead. Validation rejects PDF-position fields on evidence from such a source:

```json
{
  "sourceId": "shannon-1951-full-text",
  "locator": {
    "label": "§5, equation (17), printed p. 61",
    "section": "5. Entropy Bounds from Prediction Frequencies",
    "pageStart": 61,
    "pageEnd": 61,
    "equation": "17"
  },
  "support": "Gives the inequality connecting rank frequencies with upper and lower entropy bounds."
}
```

The `support` field explains why the location is relevant; it is not a substitute for reading the source.

A structured publisher reference list may identify a cited work for relationship evidence, but it cannot stand in for a primary-text claim locator. The validator keeps those evidence roles separate.

## Relationship discipline

Relationship direction is earlier or contributing node → later or receiving node. Each edge has:

- a `connectionType`, such as `extends` or `conceptual-parallel`;
- an `evidenceClass`: `documented-influence`, `inherited-mechanism`, or `editorial-synthesis`;
- claim IDs and direct source evidence for every relationship;
- a `causalLanguage` gate for rendering;
- confidence, rationale, and uncertainty.

Only `documented-influence` may opt into carefully qualified causal explanation. Strong causal phrases remain rejected even there: a citation documents a connection, but does not by itself prove that one work caused another. A dated-before-B relationship is not enough.

## Priority language

Claims containing terms such as “first,” “invented,” “originated,” or “proved” require a `priorityReview` with:

- an operational definition;
- the scope of the prior-art search;
- the sources supporting the conclusion.

Without that record, validation fails. Narrow wording such as “the paper constructs” or “the experiment reports” is preferred when it says exactly what the evidence supports.

## Validation

Run:

```sh
npm run validate:corpus
npm test
```

The validator checks the JSON Schema and cross-record editorial rules, including:

- complete source records and evidence locators;
- valid calendar values and source-supported date precision;
- unique IDs and valid references across works, versions, sources, ideas, claims, and objectives;
- structured version titles and ordered authors, with canonical-version identity matching the work;
- source/work/version scope, evidence pages within the cited source’s pagination, and structural-only locators for unpaginated `pdf-page` sources;
- explicit version-boundary evidence for related sources that intentionally remain outside a work’s version family and cannot support promoted claims, relationships, or chronology;
- controlled relationship and evidence labels;
- direct source evidence for documented influence;
- chronological direction for lineage edges and rejection of unsupported causal or priority wording;
- learning objectives whose cited claims belong to their stated work;
- both beginner and expert objectives for every full-treatment included work; context-only records may remain bounded prerequisites without their own paired experience.

The test suite mutates the valid corpus to prove that these failure cases are rejected.

## Expansion rule

A candidate should not be promoted by adding a title and date alone. A corpus-ready work needs:

- a primary source and version-aware bibliography;
- atomic claims with exact locators;
- limitations and material uncertainty;
- carefully typed relationships;
- distinct beginner and expert learning objectives.

The corpus schema may evolve as experiments, equations, datasets, implementations, and disputed histories require more structure. Schema changes must preserve the evidence and uncertainty already recorded.

Adding the Graves branch does not change the first planned vertical slice. That slice remains the classical smoothing sequence from Good through Katz and original Kneser–Ney to Chen and Goodman; the recurrent-generation and alignment branch remains later corpus-backed material until its own interaction and architecture gates are met.
